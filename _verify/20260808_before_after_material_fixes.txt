WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY

TRIPLANAR clears the noise floor by 8.3x on the mean (0.02480 vs 0.00298)
and the SPATIAL PATTERN MATCHES THE FEATURE'S OWN GEOMETRY:

  rock_grounding   32x noise   low camera aimed at rock
  sweep_2000       30x noise
  player_eye       11x noise
  cliff_face      8.3x noise, 59.66% of pixels changed
  diag_topdown    1.9x noise, the WEAKEST -- and it is the DISCRIMINATING
                  CONTROL, not a disappointment. Looking straight down,
                  every surface faces the camera, |N.z| ~ 1, and the
                  triplanar blend collapses to the top-down projection by
                  construction. The one camera where the feature predicts
                  near-identity is the one camera that barely moved.

_wz_axis clears the noise floor by 4.3x on the mean (0.01274 vs 0.00298),
and its pattern likewise matches its mechanism -- the forest-floor tint
band (:1987) reads world-Z, so cameras spanning altitude move and cameras
at one elevation do not:

  rock_grounding    18x noise
  sweep_2000       9.3x noise
  verify_ground    5.0x noise, 84.95% of pixels changed
  forest_floor     3.1x noise
  cliff_face       1.2x noise  -- NO CHANGE, single elevation, rock wall
  sweep_0060      BELOW noise  -- NO CHANGE

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH, AND IT IS THE IMPORTANT PART

1. NOT that the cliffs look BETTER. This proves the frames DIFFER and that
   the difference is where each mechanism predicts. Whether the result is
   the right LOOK is a judgement, and it is Ryan's, not a statistic's.
2. NOT the GPU cost. Rock albedo went 1 sample -> 3 on an iGPU already at
   75-94 ms. Still unmeasured, still needs an attended viewport reading.
3. The triplanar pair spans TWO SESSIONS two days apart; the noise floor is
   WITHIN one session. A cross-session floor could be larger than 0.00298.
   The diag_topdown control is what carries this result, not the magnitude.
